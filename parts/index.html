Notes:
  • 1482S (505–512 brushroll) — still sold under its original number; it was never renumbered into the modern K- series.
  • K-151584 (2HD brushroll) — dimensionally identical to the 1HD roll (K-152581), but adds a magnet for the Brush Performance Indicator light on late Heritage II / Legend I models; the magnet is the only reason it carries a different number.
  • K-190062 (505 – San. VII bag) — the current generic replacement bag. Kirby consolidated the original per-model 5xx–VII bags into one SKU rather than reissue each, so these models all share one bag today (DS50 onward kept distinct numbers).
  • Bag column lists OEM 3-pack part numbers; the 3CB (K-19067903) and 1HD (K-19068103) numbers are genuinely unique. Tradition — Legend II also accept generic Style 1 / 2 / 3 bags (see the filter-type table above), so the model-specific SKU isn't the only option.

Service Tools

Free, make-your-own 3D models of vintage Kirby factory service tools — every one reproducible at home from standard bar, rod, or flat stock with a lathe and a saw (no castings, no welds). Click a preview to explore it in 3D, or grab the CAD files to print or machine your own.

T104 — Armature Lock Pin

A double-ended lock pin. Push either reduced tip through the motor armature’s cross-hole to stop it turning while you break loose or torque the fan nut or brushroll — either end works. Turned from 8 mm rod, with a 3 mm dowel-pin tip pressed into each drilled end.

T123 — Handle-Spring Winder

A T-handle torque spanner for setting handle-spring tension. The dark tang under the disc engages the slot in the handle-spring shaft, letting you wind the spring against its force — a job that slips and chews up the slot if attempted with pliers. Rod, dowel pin, and bar stock, friction-fit with oil-tolerant Loctite 243.

T127 — Armature Bearing Press

A portal-frame (“arch”) press for the motor-shaft bearing. The armature shaft seats in the U-slot of the bolt-on base plate; turning the 1/4-20 pusher bolt drives the bearing squarely on or off. Milled from bar stock, with four screws holding the base plate — no castings, no welds.

Bearing service: the T127 presses a bearing off the armature shaft; to press one back on, a 5/16" socket makes a perfect driver — its lip matches the bearing’s inner race (so you drive the race, never the balls) and its bore clears the few mm of shaft that pokes through. Chill the shaft end in liquid nitrogen, slip the bearing on by hand while it’s shrunk, then tap it home with a rubber mallet on the socket until it’s fully seated.

T130 — Armature Lock (sheet steel)

A folded flat-steel armature lock from the shakeout-bag era. Its flat blade slides into a slot machined across the armature’s core laminations — between the field windings, not into the commutator (as the grainy reference photos make it look) — while the upturned tab at the far end catches the motor housing, so torque on the fan or brushroll locks against the case instead of turning the armature. A trapezoidal notch indexes it into position; the handle sticks out the back of the motor. Kirby etched that lamination slot on purpose: the machine and its service tool were designed together. Cut from 1 mm strip (laser or by hand), folded on the bend lines.
